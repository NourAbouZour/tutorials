Speed up your Wi-Fi
with Claude

I asked Claude to find everything slowing my Wi-Fi down. It found 12 fixes, and my speed went from 22 Mbps to 45 in under 10 minutes. Then it found 2 devices on my Wi-Fi I didn't recognize. Here's how to do the same, step by step.

What this guide will help you do

Claude checks your computer and your router like a network technician would. It tests your speed, finds apps eating your internet in the background, checks if your neighbours' Wi-Fi is clashing with yours, and lists every fix with a simple explanation. You say yes or no to each one. Then it finds every device connected to your Wi-Fi, so you can kick out the ones that shouldn't be there.

  • Only do this on your own Wi-Fi and router, or with the owner's permission.

Part 1: make your Wi-Fi faster

1

Test your speed now

2 minutes

You need a "before" number to know if the fixes worked.

  1. Sit where you normally use the internet.
  2. Go to speedtest.net or fast.com and run a test.
  3. Write down your download speed, upload speed and ping.

Claude also runs its own speed test in the next step, but your own number is a good check.

2

Ask Claude to find what's slowing you down

5 minutes · Claude does the work
  1. Create a folder on your computer called WiFi Check.
  2. Open the Claude desktop app, click the Code tab, start a Local session, and choose that folder.
  3. Paste this prompt. The first line is the one from the reel. The rest makes sure Claude explains everything and asks before changing anything.
COPY THIS PROMPTFind what's slowing my Wi-Fi
Connect to my router and find everything that's slowing my Wi-Fi down.

I'm a beginner. Please:
1. Find my router's address and tell me how to open its page.
2. Run a speed test and check my Wi-Fi signal strength and
   which band I'm on (2.4 GHz or 5 GHz).
3. Find every app on this computer using the internet in the
   background, and how much each one uses.
4. Check the Wi-Fi networks around me and whether my
   neighbours' Wi-Fi is on the same channel as mine.
5. Check my Wi-Fi driver, DNS and router settings.
6. Give me a numbered list of fixes, easiest first. For each:
   what it does in one simple sentence, and how much it should help.

Don't change anything yet. When you need my router password,
tell me and I'll type it myself in the router's login page.
3

Log in to your router

1 minute

Your router has its own settings page, like a small website inside your home. Claude finds its address (usually something like 192.168.1.1 or 192.168.0.1).

  1. Open that address in your browser. If Claude is connected to Chrome (the Claude in Chrome extension), it can open the page for you.
  2. When the login box appears, type the admin password from the sticker yourself. That's the only thing you have to do.
  3. Tell Claude "I'm logged in", and it carries on.
Keep your password out of the chat

Type passwords only into the router's own login page, never into the chat. And if your router still uses the password from the sticker, change it in Part 3.

4

Say yes to the fixes

5 minutes

Claude shows you its list. For me it found 12 fixes. Yours will be different. Read each one and say yes or no. If you're not sure, ask "What does this do?" first. These are the most common fixes and what they mean:

FixWhat it does
Change the Wi-Fi channelIf your neighbours use the same channel, your Wi-Fi signals bump into each other. Moving to a quieter channel can make a big difference. On 2.4 GHz, the best channels are usually 1, 6 or 11.
Use the 5 GHz band5 GHz is much faster at short range and less crowded. 2.4 GHz goes further through walls but is slower.
Stop background appsCloud sync, updates, game launchers and video apps can use your internet without you knowing. Claude pauses them or limits them.
Update the router firmwareThe router's own software. Updates fix bugs and security problems.
Update the Wi-Fi driverThe software that runs your computer's Wi-Fi card. Old drivers can be slow.
Change DNSDNS turns website names into addresses. A faster DNS makes pages start loading sooner.
Restart the routerClears out problems that build up over weeks. Simple, but it often helps.
Move the routerPut it high, central and in the open, away from thick walls, mirrors and microwaves. Not inside a cupboard.
Write down the old settings

Before you say yes, ask: "Save a note in this folder of every setting you change, with the old value, so I can undo it." Then you can always go back.

Part 2: the privacy trick

6

Find every device on your Wi-Fi

3 minutes

Someone using your Wi-Fi without permission slows it down and can be a security risk. Paste this:

COPY THIS PROMPTWho's on my Wi-Fi
Now show me every device connected to my Wi-Fi and flag anything you don't recognize.

For each device, show its name, its type if you can tell
(phone, laptop, TV...), its maker, and when it was last seen.
Put the ones you can't identify at the top.

Claude found 2 devices on my Wi-Fi that weren't identified. Before you block anything, check it isn't yours:

  • Walk around your home and list everything that uses Wi-Fi: phones, laptops, TVs, consoles, speakers, cameras, smart plugs, printers.
  • Phones often use a private Wi-Fi address that changes, so your own phone can look unknown. On your phone, open the Wi-Fi network's details to see its address and compare.
  • Turn a device off. If an "unknown" one disappears from the list, you found it.
7

Keep yours, block the rest

2 minutes
COPY THIS PROMPTBlock unknown devices
These devices are mine: LIST THE ONES YOU KNOW.
Block every other device from my Wi-Fi in my router settings.
Tell me where the setting is, so I can unblock one later.
8

Lock your Wi-Fi down

5 minutes · the most important step

Blocking a device works, but a determined person can get around it. If someone was on your Wi-Fi, they know your password. Fix the cause:

COPY THIS PROMPTSecure my Wi-Fi
Help me secure my Wi-Fi, one step at a time, asking before each change:
1. Change my Wi-Fi password to a strong new one. I'll type it myself.
2. Use WPA3 security if my router supports it, or WPA2 if not.
3. Turn off WPS (the push-button connection).
4. Change the router admin password from the one on the sticker.
   I'll type it myself.
5. Check for a router firmware update.
Then tell me which devices will need the new Wi-Fi password.

After you change the Wi-Fi password, every device needs to reconnect with the new one. Write it down somewhere safe.

Fixes

ProblemWhat to do
Claude can't find the routerMake sure your computer is on that Wi-Fi (not on a phone hotspot or a VPN). Turn off any VPN and try again.
The sticker password doesn't workSomeone may have changed it. Ask your internet provider, or find "reset router" in its manual (this erases all settings).
The internet dropped after a changeWait 2 minutes for the router to restart. If it doesn't come back, ask Claude to undo the last change using your saved note.
Speed didn't improveTest with a cable plugged into the router. If that's also slow, the problem is your internet plan or provider, not Wi-Fi. Call them.
A device won't connect anymoreCheck that you didn't block it by mistake, then forget the network on that device and reconnect with the new password.
My router page looks differentEvery brand is different. Tell Claude your router's brand and model (it's on the sticker) and ask where the setting is.

Your checklist

  • I wrote down my speed before
  • Claude listed every fix and explained each one
  • I saved a note of the old settings
  • I said yes only to fixes I understood
  • I tested again and compared
  • I checked every device on my Wi-Fi
  • I blocked the devices that aren't mine
  • I changed my Wi-Fi password and router admin password
  • WPA2 or WPA3 is on, and WPS is off

Mini glossary

RouterThe box that creates your Wi-Fi and connects your home to the internet.
MbpsMegabits per second: how fast data downloads. Higher is better.
PingHow fast your connection responds, in milliseconds. Lower is better, especially for calls and games.
ChannelA lane your Wi-Fi uses. Neighbours on the same lane slow each other down.
2.4 GHz / 5 GHzThe two Wi-Fi bands. 2.4 reaches further, 5 is faster.
FirmwareThe software running inside your router.
DNSThe internet's phone book: it turns website names into addresses.
MAC addressA device's network ID. Routers use it to recognize or block devices.
WPA2 / WPA3Wi-Fi security types. WPA3 is newest and strongest.
