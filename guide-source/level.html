The 3 levels of AI
and how to level up

Level 1 is chatbots: you ask, it answers, you copy-paste. Level 2 is agents: the AI connects to Gmail, HubSpot and Slack and does the work itself. Level 3 is agent teams: a group of AI agents running a whole department, with a supervisor checking their work. This guide takes you from level 1 to level 3, step by step.

LevelComplete beginner
Time1 afternoon
You needA Claude account

Level 2 works in the normal Claude app. Level 3 uses Claude Code, in the Code tab of the Claude desktop app. No coding needed.

What this guide will help you do

Find out which level you're on, then climb. You'll connect Claude to the tools you already use so it stops being a chat window and starts doing real work. Then you'll build your first AI team for one department (sales is the example), with one agent that qualifies leads, one that books calls, one that runs campaigns, and a supervisor above them that checks everything before it reaches you.

Level 1: chatbots, done right

1

Give the AI context, not just questions

10 minutes · do this once

Most people stay stuck on level 1 because the AI doesn't know anything about them. Fix that first. In Claude, create a Project called My Business and paste this into its instructions:

FILL THIS INBusiness brief
MY BUSINESS: what you sell, to whom, and your price range
MY CUSTOMERS: who they are, what they want, what stops them buying
MY TOOLS: e.g. Gmail, HubSpot, Slack, Instagram, Google Sheets
MY VOICE: e.g. friendly, direct, short sentences, no jargon
MY GOAL THIS QUARTER: e.g. 30 new clients
WHAT TAKES MOST OF MY TIME: e.g. replying to leads, follow-ups, posting

Every chat inside that project now starts with this knowledge. You'll reuse this brief for levels 2 and 3.

2

Find your copy-paste jobs

10 minutes

These are the tasks to hand to agents in level 2. Ask:

COPY THIS PROMPTFind what to automate
Based on my business brief, list the 10 tasks I most likely do every
week by copying and pasting between AI and other apps. For each:
how much time it probably takes, which tool it touches, and whether
an AI agent could do it with my approval. Rank them by time saved.

Pick the top 3. Those are your first agent tasks.

Level 2: give your AI hands and legs

3

Connect Claude to your tools

10 minutes

Connectors let Claude read and work in your other apps, safely, with your permission.

  1. Open Claude (web or desktop app) and go to Settings → Connectors.
  2. Browse the list and connect the tools you use: for example Gmail and Google Calendar, Slack, and your CRM such as HubSpot. If a tool isn't listed, search the connector directory for it.
  3. Each one asks you to sign in and approve what Claude can access. Read it before you click Allow.
  4. In a chat, open the tools menu in the message box to turn connectors on or off for that chat.
Start with read-only jobs

For your first week, ask Claude to read and summarize, and to draft but not send. Once you trust its work, let it do more.

4

Your first agent tasks

5 minutes each

Same AI, no copy-paste. Try these inside your My Business project:

COPY THIS PROMPTInbox
Read my Gmail from the last 24 hours. Group the emails into: needs my
reply today, can wait, and newsletters or spam. For each one that needs
a reply, write a draft in my voice and save it in Gmail drafts. Don't send anything.
COPY THIS PROMPTCRM
Check HubSpot for leads that haven't been contacted in 7 days. List them
with their last activity, and draft a short follow-up for each. Update the
"next step" field for each lead after I approve the list.
COPY THIS PROMPTTeam update
Summarize this week's new leads, booked calls and deals from HubSpot,
and write a short team update. Show it to me first, then post it in
the #sales channel on Slack once I say OK.
5

Make it run without you

Optional · 10 minutes

Level 2 becomes real when tasks happen on their own. In the Claude desktop app you can create scheduled tasks that run a prompt every morning or every week. Ask Claude: "Turn my inbox prompt into a scheduled task that runs every weekday at 8am." Then you just check the results with your coffee.

Level 3: build your AI team

One agent doing everything gets confused, like one employee doing sales, marketing and support at the same time. A team splits the work: each agent has one job, its own instructions and only the tools it needs. A supervisor checks their work before anything reaches a customer or you.

6

Plan your team on paper

15 minutes · the step most people skip

Pick one department to start with. Here's the sales team from the reel:

AgentIts one jobTools it may use
🎯 Lead qualifierReads new leads and scores each one: hot, warm or cold, and why.CRM (read, add notes), forms
📞 Call bookerWrites to hot leads and offers times for a sales call.Gmail (drafts), Calendar
📣 Campaign runnerPlans and drafts the campaign for warm leads: emails and posts.Gmail (drafts), social drafts
👑 SupervisorGives out the work, checks every result against your rules, and sends you one daily report.Reads everything, sends nothing without you
COPY THIS PROMPTDesign my team
Using my business brief, design an AI agent team for my sales / marketing / support
department. For each agent: its one job, what it reads, what it can
change, what it must never do, and what "good work" looks like.
Add a supervisor that checks their work and reports to me daily.
Keep it to 3–4 agents.
7

Build the team in Claude Code

30–60 minutes · Claude builds it

Claude Code lets you create subagents: helper agents, each with its own instructions and tools, that a main agent hands work to. Your main session becomes the supervisor.

  1. Create a folder called Sales Team and put your business brief and your team plan inside it as text files.
  2. In the Claude desktop app, open the Code tab, start a Local session and choose that folder.
  3. Paste this prompt:
COPY THIS PROMPTBuild my agent team
I'm a beginner. Build my AI sales team from the team plan in this folder.

1. Create one subagent for each role (lead qualifier, call booker,
   campaign runner), each with clear instructions, only the tools
   it needs, and the rules it must never break.
2. You are the supervisor. Hand each job to the right subagent,
   check its work against my business brief, and fix or reject
   anything that doesn't meet the rules.
3. Connect to my tools (Gmail, Calendar, HubSpot, Slack) and tell me
   step by step how to give access. Never ask me to paste
   passwords in this chat.
4. Nothing goes to a customer without my approval: emails stay as
   drafts and bookings wait for my OK.
5. Write a file called HOW-IT-WORKS.md that explains the team in
   simple words, and how to start, pause and change it.

Explain each step in one sentence before you do it.
Where the agents live

Claude Code saves each subagent as a small text file in a .claude/agents folder inside your project. Open one to read or change its instructions. It's plain English, not code.

8

Run it every day

10 minutes a day

Each morning, open your Sales Team folder in Claude Code and say:

COPY THIS PROMPTDaily run
Run today's sales cycle. Have the qualifier score new leads, the call
booker draft replies to hot leads with 3 call times from my calendar,
and the campaign runner prepare today's emails for warm leads.
Check all of it, then give me one report: what's ready for my
approval, what you rejected and why, and today's numbers.

Approve the drafts you like, and give feedback on the rest: "The call booker is too pushy. Make it friendlier and update its instructions." Every piece of feedback makes the team better. Once you trust it, ask Claude to make the daily run a scheduled task.

The same idea works for every department

Marketing

Researcher, content writer, designer brief, post scheduler. The supervisor checks brand voice.

Customer support

Inbox sorter, answer drafter, refund checker. The supervisor flags angry customers to you.

Recruiting

CV screener, interview scheduler, follow-up writer. The supervisor checks fairness rules.

Finance admin

Invoice reader, reminder drafter, monthly summary. The supervisor checks every number.

Safety rules for agents
  • Approve before anything goes out. Emails, posts, bookings and CRM changes that customers see should wait for your OK until you fully trust them.
  • Give each agent only the access it needs. The campaign runner doesn't need your calendar.
  • Test on yourself first. Send test emails to your own address before real customers.
  • Respect privacy and the law. Only email people who agreed to hear from you, and follow your country's data and marketing rules.
  • Never paste passwords or API keys into a chat. Use the connector sign-in screens.
  • Check the numbers. AI can make mistakes. Your supervisor agent helps, but you're still the boss.

Fixes

ProblemWhat to do
I can't find a connector for my toolSearch the connector directory in Settings → Connectors. If it's not there, ask Claude Code to help you connect it another way, or export the data to a sheet.
The agent did something I didn't wantUndo it, then add a rule: "Never do X without asking me." Put rules in the agent's instructions, not only in the chat.
The emails don't sound like meGive it 5 emails you wrote yourself and say: "Write exactly like these."
Agents step on each other's workMake each job smaller and clearer, and let only the supervisor hand out work.
It's too much at onceGo back to one agent and one task. Add the next agent only when the first works well.
Scheduled tasks don't runYour computer may be asleep or the app closed. Ask Claude how to keep it running at that time.

Mini glossary

ChatbotAn AI you talk to. It answers, but it can't act in your other apps.
AgentAn AI that can use tools and take actions, like reading email or updating a CRM.
Agent teamSeveral agents, each with one job, working together under a supervisor.
Supervisor agentThe agent that hands out work and checks it before it reaches you.
SubagentA helper agent in Claude Code with its own instructions and tools.
ConnectorA secure link that lets Claude work inside another app, like Gmail or HubSpot.
CRMCustomer relationship management: where you track leads and customers, like HubSpot.
Lead qualificationDeciding how likely a lead is to buy, so you spend time on the best ones.
