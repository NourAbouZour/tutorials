LevelBeginner, with patience

It runs on your Claude subscription, with no separate AI bill. You'll also need an email-sending service (explained inside).

What this guide will help you do

Finding clients usually means hours of searching for businesses, reading their websites, and writing emails one by one. Harvey is an open-source agent that does that work with Claude Code.

You tell it what you sell and who your ideal client is. It finds matching businesses, reads their websites, decides who's worth contacting, and has Claude write a personal cold email for each one. It then watches your inbox, spots who's interested, and drafts replies and follow-ups. You approve every email before it's sent.

Harvey has a new name

Harvey was made by ethanplusai on GitHub and has since been renamed Sellbird. The old link still works and takes you to the project: github.com/ethanplusai/harvey. Commands may use either name, so always follow the README.

Install Harvey

About 15 minutes
  1. Open the Claude desktop app, click the Code tab, start a new Local session, and choose a new folder called Harvey.
  2. Paste this prompt:

2

Describe your offer and ideal client

15 minutes · the most important step

Harvey is only as good as what you tell it. Be specific: "small businesses" is too vague, while "dental clinics in Dubai with 2–10 dentists and an outdated website" is great.

COPY THIS PROMPTYour offer and ideal client
Set up my Harvey profile:

What I sell: e.g. websites for dental clinics
The result I get for clients: e.g. more online bookings
Price range: e.g. $1,500-$3,000
Ideal client: industry, location, size
Signs they need me: e.g. no online booking, old website, few reviews
Who to contact: e.g. the owner or practice manager
Proof I can mention: e.g. a past result or client (only real ones)
My name and how to reach me: NAME, WEBSITE

Ask me anything that's missing, then save it as my profile.
3

Set up email sending safely

30 minutes, then 2–3 weeks of warm-up

Cold emails can damage your email reputation if you set them up wrong. Do it properly once:

  1. Buy a separate domain for outreach, and create an email address on it.
  2. Set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC. These are settings that prove your emails are really from you. Ask Claude: "Explain SPF, DKIM and DMARC simply and walk me through setting them up for my domain and email provider."
  3. Connect the address to your sending service (such as Instantly), and turn on warm-up. It slowly builds your reputation over 2–3 weeks before real sending.
  4. Connect the sending service to Harvey by following the README. Paste any keys into the place Claude tells you, never into the chat.
Don't skip the warm-up

A brand-new address that suddenly sends lots of emails goes straight to spam, and can get the domain blocked. Patience here saves you weeks of problems.

4

Run your first small campaign

Start with 20 businesses
COPY THIS PROMPTFirst campaign
Start a small test campaign with Harvey: find 20 businesses that match my ideal client.
For each one, show me: the business, why it's a good fit, who to contact, and the personal email you'd send.
Don't send anything until I approve each email.

What a good email looks like

  • Short: 4–6 sentences.
  • Personal: mentions something real about their business, not a generic compliment.
  • One clear question at the end, like "Worth a quick chat?", not a hard sell.
  • Honest: says who you are, uses no fake familiarity, and offers an easy way to opt out.

Read each draft. Edit, approve or reject it. Rejecting bad emails and explaining why ("too pushy", "wrong person") teaches it what you want.

6

Replies and follow-ups

Where the clients come from

Harvey watches your inbox, works out who's interested, and drafts your replies and follow-ups. Check the dashboard once or twice a day:

  • Interested: review the drafted reply, make it personal, approve it, and book the call.
  • Not now: a polite reply. Ask if you can follow up in a few months.
  • Not interested or "remove me": stop immediately, and make sure they're never contacted again.
The rules: this is not a spam machine
  • Approve every email before it goes out. You're responsible for what's sent in your name.
  • Only contact relevant businesses, ones with a real reason to care about your offer. Never blast everyone.
  • Keep volume low, especially at the start, for example 20–30 emails a day per address.
  • Follow the law where you and they are. Cold email rules (like CAN-SPAM in the US, and GDPR and PECR in Europe and the UK) require things like honest sender details, a way to opt out, and respecting "no". Check what applies to you.
  • Never buy email lists or invent facts, results or reviews.

Your checklist

  • Harvey is installed from the GitHub link, following the README
  • My offer and ideal client profile are specific
  • I use a separate outreach domain with SPF, DKIM and DMARC set up
  • My sending address finished warming up
  • I ran a 20-business test and approved each email myself
  • I can open the dashboard and see leads, emails and conversations
  • I stop instantly for anyone who says no
