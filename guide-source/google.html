How Google search ads work

When someone searches on Google, the first results marked "Sponsored" are ads. Businesses pick keywords (the searches they want to show up for), write a short ad, and choose a landing page: the page people arrive on after clicking. You usually pay only when someone clicks.

1. KeywordSomeone searches "dentist near me".
→
2. AdYour headline and description show up.
→
3. ClickYou pay for the click (CPC).
→
4. Landing pageThe page has to convince them.
→
5. ConversionThey call, book or buy.

Each step can win or lose you money. That's why we improve all four: keywords, ads, landing page and budget.

Option B: the free method, no skill needed

Works in any Claude chat

You don't need a special tool to see your competitors' ads. Google shows them publicly, and Claude can do all the thinking. These are the two free sources you'll use:

Google Ads Transparency Center

Free and public, at adstransparency.google.com. Search any advertiser to see the ads they're running, with their headlines and text.

Google Keyword Planner

Free inside your Google Ads account, under Tools → Keyword Planner. It shows what people search and roughly how much clicks cost.

The steps below work with both options. With the skill, Claude collects the data for you. Without it, you copy the ads and keywords in yourself.

15 minutes
  1. With the skill: ask Claude to collect the current Google ads for each competitor.
  2. Without it: open adstransparency.google.com, search each competitor, set the format to Text (search ads), and copy their headlines and descriptions into a document. Note which page each ad links to.
COPY THIS PROMPTCompetitor analysis
Here are the Google ads my competitors are running right now:
PASTE THEIR HEADLINES, DESCRIPTIONS AND LANDING PAGE LINKS

Analyse them like an ads expert:
1. The offers and hooks they repeat most (these are usually the ones that work)

Long-running ads are clues

An ad a competitor has kept running for months is probably making them money. Pay extra attention to those.

Build your keyword list

15 minutes

In Google Ads, open Tools → Keyword Planner → Discover new keywords, enter your product and website, and download the results as a file. Then give it to Claude:

COPY THIS PROMPTKeywords
Here is my Keyword Planner export: ATTACH THE FILE OR PASTE THE LIST

Build my keyword plan:
1. Group keywords into 3-5 tight themes (each will be its own ad group)
2. Keep only keywords from people ready to buy, not just browsing
3. Suggest the match type for each (phrase or exact for a small budget)
4. A list of negative keywords to block wasted clicks (like "free", "jobs", "DIY", if they don't fit me)
5. Flag the keywords that are too expensive for my budget of $__ per day
Put it in a table.

Write and improve

6

Write your ads

10 minutes per ad group

Google's main search ad format is the Responsive Search Ad (RSA). You give it up to 15 headlines (30 characters each) and 4 descriptions (90 characters each), and Google tests the combinations for you.

COPY THIS PROMPTAd writer
Write a Responsive Search Ad for the ad group "THEME", using everything we learned about my business and my competitors.

- 15 headlines, each 30 characters or less (show the count)
- 4 descriptions, each 90 characters or less (show the count)
- Include: the main keyword, my top selling points, a clear offer, and a call to action
- Make at least 3 headlines say something my competitors don't
- No claims I can't prove, no fake urgency, no ALL CAPS
Then suggest 4 sitelinks and 4 callouts.
Stay inside Google's rules

Google rejects ads with exaggerated claims, excessive punctuation or misleading offers. Everything in your ad must also be true on your landing page.

7

Fix your landing page

The step most people skip

A great ad that sends people to a confusing page wastes your money. Before you spend anything, check the page:

COPY THIS PROMPTLanding page audit
Audit this landing page for people clicking my Google ad about "THEME": PAGE LINK

Score it out of 10 on:
1. Does the headline match what they searched?
2. Is the offer and price clear within 5 seconds?
3. Is there one obvious action (call, book, buy)?
4. Trust: reviews, photos, guarantees, contact details
5. Speed and how it looks on a phone
Then write the exact new text for the headline, the first paragraph and the button, so I can copy them in.

Budget and a small starter plan

Start small, learn, then grow
WhenWhat to do
Week 1–2One campaign, 1–2 ad groups, your best keywords only, a small daily budget you can afford to lose while learning.
Week 3–4Pause keywords that get clicks but no results. Add negative keywords. Keep the best headlines.
Month 2Move budget toward the ad groups that bring calls, bookings or sales. Test one new landing page change at a time.
COPY THIS PROMPTBudget plan
My total budget is $___ per month. Based on my keyword costs, suggest how to split it between my ad groups, a daily budget, and the number of clicks I can realistically expect. Show the maths and tell me what's too ambitious for this budget.
No guarantees

Nobody can promise ad results, not even Claude. Prices per click change all the time, and every market is different. The goal is to spend smarter and learn faster.

Mini glossary

CPCCost per click: what you pay each time someone clicks your ad.
CTRClick-through rate: the percentage of people who see your ad and click it.
ConversionThe action you want, like a call, a booking or a sale.
RSAResponsive Search Ad: you give many headlines and descriptions, and Google mixes them.
Match typesHow closely a search must match your keyword: broad, phrase or exact.
Negative keywordA word you block so your ad doesn't show for it (like "free").
Quality ScoreGoogle's 1–10 rating of how relevant your keyword, ad and landing page are. Higher usually means cheaper clicks.
