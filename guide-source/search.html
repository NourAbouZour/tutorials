What this guide will help you do

When someone searches "best dentist in Beirut" or "wedding photographer near me", Google often shows an AI answer at the top. It names a few businesses and gives a short reason for each. Lots of people read that answer and stop there.

If the AI answer names your competitor and not you, you've lost that client before they ever saw your website. The good news: AI picks businesses based on things you can improve, like your reviews, your service pages and your answers to the questions clients ask. Claude can check all of that for you and help you fix it.

Be realistic

Nobody controls what Google's AI says, and nothing can guarantee you'll be mentioned. What you can do is give AI every good reason to pick you. That's what this guide covers.

How AI decides who to mention

Google doesn't publish exactly how its AI answers are made, and it changes them often. But the AI answer is built from what Google can find and trust about you online. In practice, these are the signals that matter most:

Reviews

How many you have, how good they are, how recent they are, and whether you reply to them.

Clear service pages

A page for each service, saying exactly what you do and where. AI can't recommend you for something your site doesn't mention.

Answers to real questions

"How much does it cost?", "Do you take walk-ins?" If your site answers these, AI can quote you.

Consistent details everywhere

The same name, address and phone number on your site, Google, Facebook, directories and maps.

The 12-check AI visibility audit

About 20–30 minutes while Claude works

This prompt asks Claude to send out 12 checks at once, like a team of 12 agents each looking at one thing. Fill in the yellow parts and paste it all.

COPY THIS PROMPT12-check audit
You are my AI search consultant. Check how AI search sees my business.

My business: BUSINESS NAME
Website: https://YOURSITE.com (or "none")
Google Business Profile link: PASTE IT
Main services: SERVICE 1, SERVICE 2, SERVICE 3
City / area: YOUR CITY
Competitors: COMPETITOR 1, COMPETITOR 2, COMPETITOR 3

Run these 12 checks in parallel (use sub-agents if you can).
Score each one from 0 to 10 and say why:

1. Google Business Profile: categories, hours, services,
   description, photos, website link. What's missing?
2. Reviews: count, average rating, how recent, and whether
   the owner replies. Compare with my competitors.
3. Service pages: is there one clear page for each service
   and area? List the missing pages.
4. FAQ: does my site answer the questions clients really ask
   (prices, timing, booking, location, parking)? List gaps.
5. NAP: is my name, address and phone exactly the same on my
   site, Google, Facebook, Instagram and directories?
6. Structured data: does my site have LocalBusiness and FAQ
   schema? Is it correct?
7. AI answers: ask "best SERVICE in CITY" and 2 similar
   questions. Record which businesses AI search mentions and
   whether I appear. Say which tool you used for this.
8. Competitors: what do the businesses that ARE mentioned
   have that I don't?
9. First screen: does my homepage say what I do, where,
   and for whom, within 5 seconds?
10. Mobile and speed: any obvious problems on a phone?
11. Mentions: am I mentioned on other trusted sites
    (local news, directories, blogs, partner sites)?
12. Freshness: when were my last Google posts and photos?

Then give me:
- A total score out of 120 and a table of the 12 scores
- The 10 most important fixes, ranked by impact and effort
- Which fixes you can do for me, and which I must do myself
Save the full report as audit-.md in this folder.
Don't change anything on my website or Google profile.
If Claude can't open something

Some sites (and Google Maps) block automated visits. If Claude says it can't see your Google profile or reviews, take screenshots or copy the text into a file in your folder and say: "Use the files in this folder for checks 1, 2 and 12."

Fix what's missing

4

Get more real reviews

The strongest signal for most local businesses
  1. Get your review link: in your Google Business Profile, find Ask for reviews (or "Share review form") and copy the link.
  2. Ask every happy client, right after a good experience. A short, personal message works best.
  3. Reply to every review, good and bad, within a few days.
COPY THIS PROMPTReview requests
Write 3 short, friendly messages I can send on WhatsApp to happy clients asking for a Google review. My business: NAME AND SERVICE. Include this link: REVIEW LINK
Also write 5 reply templates for reviews: 3 positive, 1 mixed, 1 negative. Keep them warm and personal, with a spot for the client's name and what they mentioned.
Never fake or buy reviews

Fake reviews, paid reviews and reviews from staff or family break Google's rules, and in many countries the law too. Google removes them and can suspend your profile. Only ask real clients, and never offer something in exchange for a positive review.

5

One page per service

So AI knows exactly what you do

A single "Services" page with a list isn't enough. Each main service deserves its own page that says what it is, who it's for, the price range, how it works, where you offer it, and a few common questions.

COPY THIS PROMPTService page
Write a service page for "SERVICE in CITY" for my business NAME.
Include: a clear first sentence saying what we do and where, who it's for, what's included, price range (YOUR PRICES), how booking works, why clients choose us (only use these real facts: FACTS), and 5 FAQs with short answers.
Simple English, under 600 words. Mark anything I need to check with [CHECK]. Save it as a file. Don't publish anything.
6

Answer your clients' questions

AI loves quotable answers
  1. Write down the 10 questions you get most in DMs, calls and WhatsApp.
  2. Ask Claude to turn them into a clear FAQ section, with a short direct answer to each one.
  3. Add it to your website, and ask Claude to add FAQ schema (code that helps search engines read it). If you're not technical, ask: "Give me the schema code and tell me exactly where to paste it in WordPress / Wix / Shopify."
7

Polish your Google Business Profile

30 minutes, then 10 minutes a week
  • Choose the most accurate main category, then add secondary ones.
  • Fill in every field: services, hours, description, area served, booking link.
  • Add real photos: your place, your team, your work. Add new ones every month.
  • Post an update every week or two: an offer, a tip, or a recent job.
  • Make sure your name, address and phone match your website exactly.
COPY THIS PROMPTGoogle profile content
Using my audit report, write: a new Google Business Profile description (under 750 characters, no keyword stuffing), a list of services with one-line descriptions, and 4 weekly post ideas with the text ready to copy.

Re-test every month

10 minutes

Changes take time to show up in AI answers. Run the audit again once a month and compare:

COPY THIS PROMPTMonthly re-test
Run the 12-check AI visibility audit again. Compare it with my last audit file in this folder. Show what improved, what got worse, and whether AI answers mention me now. Then give me the next 5 fixes.

Your checklist

  • I ran the audit (skill or 12-check prompt) and saved the report
  • I know my score and my top 3 fixes
  • I sent review requests to real, happy clients, and I reply to every review
  • Each main service has its own page
  • My site has a FAQ answering real client questions
  • My Google Business Profile is complete, with fresh photos and posts
  • My name, address and phone match everywhere
  • I put a monthly re-test in my calendar
