What a backlink is (honestly)

A backlink is simply a link from another website to yours. When a trusted site like Crunchbase or G2 links to you, it helps in three ways:

Discovery

Google and AI tools find your site through the places that link to it.

Trust

Being listed on known sites shows you're a real, active business.

Mentions

AI answers often draw on review sites and directories, so being there gives you a chance to be named.

Being honest about "nofollow"

Many profile links are marked nofollow, which means they pass less direct ranking power. They still help people and search engines discover you, and they build mentions of your brand. Think of this list as building your presence, not as a magic ranking button. Nobody can promise you page one.

Make your business profile kit

About 20 minutes · do this once

Every site asks for the same things: a name, a description, a logo, categories. Prepare them once and copy-paste everywhere. It's faster, and your information stays exactly the same on every site, which search engines like.

COPY THIS PROMPTBusiness profile kit
Create my business profile kit for directory and review-site listings.

Business name: NAME
Website: https://YOURSITE.com
What we do: ONE OR TWO SENTENCES
Who it's for: YOUR CUSTOMERS
Location / contact: CITY, COUNTRY, EMAIL, PHONE IF PUBLIC
Founder: YOUR NAME AND ROLE

Write:
1. A tagline (under 60 characters)
2. A short description (under 160 characters)
3. A medium description (about 50 words)
4. A long description (about 150 words)
5. 5 categories and 10 tags people would search for
6. A founder bio (short and long)
7. A launch post for Product Hunt-style sites
Keep the business name, address and phone identical in every version.
Save it all in profile-kit.md.

Also get these files ready

  • Your logo as a square PNG (at least 512×512) and a wide version
  • 3–5 screenshots or photos of your product, service or place
  • A professional photo of you, for founder profiles
  • A business email on your own domain, if you have one (it looks more trustworthy)

Let your agent work the list

The "get us on all 50" part

An AI agent, like Claude Code or Claude in Chrome, can do most of the boring work: open each site, find the sign-up page, and fill in the forms from your profile kit. But be realistic about what it can't do for you:

Your agent does

Finds the right page, checks the site fits your business, fills in descriptions and categories, and keeps the tracking sheet up to date.

You do

Create the accounts, choose passwords, verify your email or phone, solve the "I'm not a robot" checks, and accept each site's terms.

COPY THIS PROMPTWork through the list
Help me get listed on the sites in this guide. Use profile-kit.md for every field.

Work through the list ONE site at a time:
1. Check the site fits my business. If it doesn't, skip it and say why.
2. Open the free sign-up or "list your business" page.
3. Fill in everything you can from my profile kit.
4. STOP and tell me whenever I need to create an account, enter a password,
   verify an email or phone, solve a CAPTCHA, or accept terms. Wait for me.
5. After it's submitted, add a row to tracking.csv: site, status,
   profile URL, link live?, date.
Never pay for anything, and never choose a paid plan.

Start with: G2 / Crunchbase / Google Business Profile
Quality over speed

Don't mass-submit to everything in one day, and never create fake reviews or fake accounts. Follow each site's rules. A few honest, complete profiles beat fifty half-empty ones.

Your 2-week plan

DaysWhat to do
Day 1Make your profile kit (Step 1). Pick the sites that fit your business and set up your tracking sheet.
Days 2–3The big ones first: Google Business Profile (if local), LinkedIn, Crunchbase, and your top review site.
Days 4–6Software review sites or agency directories, whichever fits. Several review listings take a few days to be approved.
Days 7–8Startup databases and claimed profiles (Dealroom, PitchBook, Owler, Tracxn).
Days 9–11Launch sites. Space them out, and prepare a short launch post and a few screenshots for each.

How to check it's working

  • Search your business name on Google. Over the following weeks you should see more of your profiles (Crunchbase, LinkedIn, G2…) on the first page.
  • Ask AI about you. Ask ChatGPT, Claude or Perplexity: "What is your business?" and "What are good tools for what you do?" Note down whether you're mentioned, and check again every few weeks.
  • Check your link is live. Open each profile and click your link to make sure it works.
  • If you have a website, Google Search Console (free) shows you sites that link to you, under Links.
Be patient

Search engines and AI tools update over weeks and months, not hours. Keep your profiles complete and up to date, and collect real reviews from happy customers. That's what builds trust over time.

The rules
  • Only list where you belong. A local bakery doesn't belong on G2.
  • Never fake reviews or ask friends to pretend to be customers. Sites remove them and can ban you.
  • Keep your details identical everywhere: the same name, address, phone and website.
  • Read each site's terms, and don't automate in ways a site doesn't allow.
  • You don't need paid upgrades. Free profiles are enough to start.

Fixes

ProblemWhat to do
My listing is "pending review"Normal for review sites and directories. It can take days or weeks. Note it in your sheet and check back.
The site wants paymentLook for a "free listing" or "claim your profile" option. If there isn't one, skip it. There are 49 others.
My agent got stuck on a sign-upThat's expected: accounts, verification and CAPTCHAs are your part. Do that step, then tell it to continue.
My link isn't clickableSome sites only allow links on paid plans, or show the website as text. It still counts as a brand mention.
Different sites show different detailsUpdate them all to match your profile kit exactly.
Profile already exists (made by someone else)Use the site's "claim this company" option to take control of it.

Your checklist

  • My profile kit is ready (descriptions, categories, tags, bio, logo, screenshots)
  • I listed on the big ones first (Google, LinkedIn, Crunchbase, my main review site)
  • My agent filled the forms, and I did the accounts and verifications myself
