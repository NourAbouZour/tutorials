What this guide will help you do

A brand-new website can take months before Google trusts it enough to show it on page one. But Google already trusts sites like LinkedIn, Reddit and Zenodo, and ChatGPT reads and quotes them all the time.

Parasite SEO means publishing your content on those trusted sites, so it borrows their trust. For searches with little competition, a good post can reach the top within a couple of weeks. And because AI assistants read these sites too, your answers can start appearing in ChatGPT.

The idea in plain English

Someone searches"best budgeting apps 2027" on Google, or asks ChatGPT.
→

You're not tricking anyone. You're putting a genuinely useful answer in the places where people (and AI) already look.

The golden rule: be useful, never spammy

Every one of these sites removes spam, and some ban accounts for it. Each post must actually answer the question better than what's already there. Follow each site's rules, and always be honest when you're promoting your own product. Done this way, it works long term. Done the spammy way, it gets deleted.

Find your 20 questions

About 30 minutes · do this first

Every method in this guide starts from one thing: the exact questions your customers type into Google. Build a list of 20 before you write anything.

Where to find them (all free)

  • Google autocomplete: start typing your topic in Google and write down the suggestions.
  • "People also ask": the box of questions in Google's results. Click a few and more appear.
  • Reddit: search your topic and note the questions people keep asking.
  • Your own DMs and comments: the questions your followers already ask you.
  • Ask an AI to brainstorm with you:

- "how to" questions
- "best ___ for ___" questions
- comparisons ("X vs Y")
- questions that include a year, like "2027"
Mark the 20 that are most specific (long questions usually have less competition).
Specific beats popular

"Marketing tips" is impossible to rank for. "How to get first 10 customers for a home bakery on Instagram" is easy, and the person searching it is much closer to buying.

Save your 20 questions in a spreadsheet (Google Sheets is fine). You'll use it to track everything.

Pick one to start, then add more

You don't have to do all five at once. Start with Method 3 (LinkedIn) or Method 5 (Reddit) because they're the fastest, then add the others week by week.

Write about 2027 now

Get there before everyone else

People search with the year: "best laptops for students 2027". Most creators only write those posts once the year starts. If you publish now, there's almost no competition, so the first decent page often takes the top spot and keeps it as the searches grow.

  1. Pick 3–5 of your questions and add the coming year: "best ___ in 2027", "how to ___ in 2027".
  2. Write a genuinely helpful post. Put the year in the title and the first sentence.
  3. Publish it on a trusted platform: LinkedIn articles, Medium or Substack (all free).
  4. Update it a few times a year so it stays the best answer.

Publish research on Zenodo

The one AI loves to quote

Zenodo is a free research library run by CERN (the European science lab). Anything published there gets a permanent DOI, a kind of official ID used for research. AI tools like ChatGPT treat it as a reliable source and quote it often.

What to publish

Real, small research you did yourself. For example: a survey of 50 of your followers, results from testing 10 tools, a case study of your own numbers, or a useful dataset. It doesn't need to be academic. It needs to be real and useful.

How to publish

  1. Create a free account at zenodo.org (you can sign in with GitHub or ORCID, or use email).
  2. Turn your research into a short PDF report: a title that matches a search question, a 3-line summary at the top, your method, results and conclusion.
  3. Click New upload, add the PDF, choose a type such as Publication → Report, and fill in the title, your name, a clear description and keywords.
  4. Add a link to your page or Instagram in the description, then click Publish.
It's permanent

Once published, a Zenodo record can't simply be deleted. You can only add new versions. Check everything before you click Publish, and only upload genuine research, never adverts.

3

Short LinkedIn articles

Under 330 words, answer in the first line

LinkedIn articles rank well on Google, and AI assistants like answers that come first, with no long intro. So keep each article short and direct.

  1. On LinkedIn, click Write article (on the home page, near where you'd start a post).
  2. Make the headline one of your exact questions.
  3. Write the answer in the very first sentence. Then explain it in short sections. Stay under 330 words.
  4. Add one link at the end to your Instagram, product or page, then publish.
  5. Do one article per question. Aim for 2–3 a week.

4

20 Gamma slide decks

One deck for each question

Gamma is a free AI tool that makes good-looking presentations in a minute. Public Gamma pages can show up in Google too, so one deck per question means 20 more chances to be found.

  1. Sign up free at gamma.app.
  2. Create a new presentation and give it one question as the topic. Make the title the exact question.
  3. Edit the slides so the answer is clear and correct. Add a final slide with a link to your Instagram, product or page.
  4. Use Share to make it public (anyone with the link, or publish to the web).
  5. Repeat for all 20 questions. Once you get used to it, each deck takes about 10 minutes.
5

Helpful Reddit answers

Google and ChatGPT both read Reddit

Google shows Reddit threads for huge numbers of searches, and AI tools quote Reddit answers constantly. A genuinely helpful answer in the right thread can bring visitors for years.

  1. Find threads by searching Google for: site:reddit.com your question
  2. Open threads that rank well, and read the subreddit's rules first. Many limit self-promotion.
  3. Write the most helpful answer in the thread: specific steps, real experience, no sales talk.
  4. If you mention your product, say it's yours, for example: "Full disclosure: I made this tool." Honesty builds trust and keeps you from getting banned.
  5. Answer more questions than you promote. A good habit is roughly 9 helpful answers for every 1 that mentions your product.
New account?

Brand-new Reddit accounts often can't post or get filtered. Spend a few days commenting helpfully in your topic's communities before you share any links.

Be patient and keep going

Results depend on how competitive each question is. Some posts rank in days, some take weeks, and some never do. That's why you publish many. Double down on the questions and platforms that work for you.
